# Can anything beat bb-core or I-Core? 27 indicator strategies, 5 coins, 4h and 1h, 2020 to Nov 5 2026

Written Nov 6 2026 while the owner was away. Nothing here touches the live bots or any config. Code and data are in this folder (`strat-hunt-nov6/`); every number below is regenerated by `node build-report.js`.

## Short answer

1. **Beat I-Core: yes, easily.** On 4h, 18 of 57 variants beat I-Core's +668R, and bb-core's own entry with I-Core's exact exit rules (`bb-trail P7`) makes +781R. On 1h, 40 of 57 variants beat I-Core (a 4x-scaled copy makes only +362R).
2. **Beat bb-core on total R: yes, 32 of 57 variants on 4h (up to +788R vs +594R), but not in a way I would trust.** On 1h only 7 of 57 beat bb-core (+724R), the best by +11%. The big winners all use a trailing stop. They get 66–88% of their profit from their 10 best trades (bb-core: 35%), win only 21–32% of trades, earn little in 2022–2023, and make far less on shorts (+45R to +94R vs bb-core's +235R). They are bull-market-biased, and their results jump around when a setting is nudged (no plateau). The milder change, just holding bb-core's trades longer (96 candles instead of 48), adds only +8% R for +20% drawdown. bb-core's own setting sits on a stable plateau.
3. **The thing that does beat bb-core is a pairing, not a replacement.** bb-core plus a wide-trail trend strategy at half risk each (same total risk) has about zero monthly correlation with bb-core and cuts the max drawdown: on 4h, `bb-core + fractal P14` makes 684R with a 18.7R drawdown, against 594R and 25.0R for bb-core alone. This is not cherry-picking: it improves return-to-drawdown in 21 of 22 pairings on both 4h and 1h. It survives double costs. It is mainly a smoother ride, not much more profit.
4. **Mean reversion does not work here.** The z-score dip, Bollinger fade and RSI(2) strategies all lose money after costs on both timeframes (about -0.02R per trade). The z-score and Bollinger-fade rules turned out to be the same rule (identical results), so that is two distinct ideas, not three.
5. **The exit matters more than the indicator.** On 4h, 32 of 57 variants, from completely different indicators, land between +600R and +790R once they use the ATR trail. The entry's identity is a minor effect.

## What was tested

- **Coins:** SOL, ZEC, NEAR, ADA, GALA (the bot's five). **Data:** Binance USDT-M futures, complete with no gaps, to Nov 5 2026. The 4h candles are identical to the bot's own files where they overlap. Real funding history is included (ends Oct 21–26; the last ~10 days are not charged).
  The bot's 1h folder holds MEXC data with a 62-day hole for ZEC and ADA, so I did not use it.
- **Years:** 2020–2026 (2026 to Nov 5). Not every coin has 7 years: ZEC and ADA start Jan/Feb 2020, SOL Sep 2020, NEAR Oct 2020, GALA Sep 2021. Years are by trade exit date.
- **Costs (the bot's real model):** 0.05% taker fee on entry and on every exit, 0.02% slippage on every fill, real funding. Entry is at the next candle's open after the signal. Stop is checked before anything else in a candle (worst case). 1R = the stop distance, so every strategy is risk-normalised.
- **Strategies (27 new ones plus the two live bots, 118 variants once exit styles are counted):** oscillators (RSI momentum, RSI dip, Stochastic RSI, CCI, Awesome Oscillator, Elder Impulse, Fisher), momentum (time-series momentum, KST, Heikin-Ashi), trend (EMA ribbon, Ichimoku TK cross, rolling VWAP, OBV), breakouts (Donchian + volume, efficiency-ratio breakout, choppiness breakout, fractal break, momentum candle), four bb-core variants (volume filter, ADX filter, squeeze filter, ATR-trail exit), a hybrid that keeps bb-core's Bollinger-middle stop and adds a trail or longer hold, and three mean-reversion ideas. All use the same trend rule as the bots (trade with the 200-SMA). Indicator settings are the plain textbook ones, **not tuned per coin**.
- **Exits:** `P7` = 2 ATR stop and a 7 ATR trail from the best price (I-Core's rule); `P14` = same with a 14 ATR trail; `BBX` = bb-core's own (Bollinger-middle stop, 48-candle time exit). On 1h all lookbacks are x4 (same time horizon, like bb-core-1h) and the ATR multiples x2.
- **Trust checks:**
  - The new engine reproduces the real `lucidio-core.js` **trade for trade on all 5 coins** with costs off (0 mismatches, R differences below 1e-10) and the **same total with real costs and funding: 594.2R both**. I-Core matches trade for trade too; its total differs slightly (668R vs 654R) only because the real I-Core charges a flat funding guess.
  - **No look-ahead:** every strategy was recomputed on data cut at random bars (5,040 comparisons, 0 mismatches).

## Why I would not replace bb-core with any of them

How much of each result is real? (4h; "best 10" = share of total R from the 10 best trades; shorts shows how well it earns in a falling market.)

{{ROBUST4H}}

- The high-total strategies are **lottery-shaped**: win rate about 21% and 66–88% of profit from 10 trades. Remove those 10 trades and `don-vol P14` falls from +750R to +87R, `bb-trail P14` from +788R to +147R. bb-core falls from +594R to +384R.
- They are **long-biased**. bb-core earned +235R on shorts (+112R of it in 2022); the P14 trail variants earned only +45R to +94R on shorts. Because the market moved up most of 2020–2025, a strategy that rides longs scores higher in total R.
- The 95% bootstrap interval of their per-trade expectancy is very wide (`fractal P14`: +0.32R to +2.45R) and mostly overlaps bb-core's (+0.29R to +0.60R), so most of the extra R cannot be separated from luck. (Per-trade R is not strictly comparable across different exit styles; total R and drawdown are the fairer yardsticks.)
- The milder hold-extensions are not lottery-shaped (`bb-hybrid BBX96`: 46% of profit from the best 10 trades, shorts +184R) but they buy little: +639R vs +594R with a drawdown of 29.9R vs 25.0R, so return-to-drawdown is slightly worse (21.4 vs 23.8).

- Total R rises in 14 of 22 pairings on 4h but only 6 of 22 on 1h, so the benefit is **mostly lower drawdown**, not more profit.

The price: in 2022–2023 the pair earns less than bb-core alone (e.g. 4h `+ fractal P14`: 2022 +48R vs +81R, 2023 +39R vs +70R), and it needs a second strategy running on the same coins (more margin and orders).

## Stress test: doubled costs

Every number above survives doubling fees and slippage. On 4h, bb-core goes from 594R to 554R (-7%), the wide-trail strategies lose only 2–3%. The pairing holds: `bb-core + fractal P14` 33.2 R/DD vs bb-core alone 21.5 (4h), 29.5 vs 20.2 (1h). Wide trails trade less often and are barely touched by costs.

## Caveats (read these)

- **This is a backtest on 5 coins that mostly went up 2020–2025.** Long-biased trend riders look better than they will in a long bear market, and the sample has few independent bull/bear cycles.
- **I looked at 118 variants.** Some of the top of any leaderboard is selection luck. That is why the conclusions lean on family-wide effects (21/22 pairings, plateau scans, double costs) and not on the single best row.
- **R is not dollars.** R is risk-normalised, but account growth depends on how much of the account is risked per trade and on compounding. Drawdowns in R were measured trade by trade (closed trades only, no intra-trade equity).
- **Margin and concurrency were not modelled.** One position per coin per strategy; running two strategies on the same coin means two positions.
- **1h parameters are my x4 scaling**, not tuned. The bot's own 1h folder uses MEXC data with holes, so numbers will differ from the 1h paper bot.
- **Funding for the last ~10 days (after Oct 21–26) is not charged** (the file ends there).

## Suggested next steps (nothing was changed)

1. **Keep bb-core as it is.** No tested replacement is more robust.
2. If a second strategy is wanted, **paper-test a wide-trail sleeve alongside it** (for example `fractal P14` or `don-vol P14` on 4h, risked at half of what bb-core's slot uses), not on live money first.
3. Do not extend bb-core's 48-candle exit based on this: it trades bear-market income for bull-market income and doubles the drawdown.
4. If you want a follow-up: walk-forward the pairing (fit on 2020–2022, test 2023+), and a margin-aware portfolio simulation with the real account size.
