Running the BB Breakout bot with real money:
every risk we know of, and how each one is prevented

This document lists 46 risks in 7 groups — from the strategy itself to the bot, the exchange, security, our own behaviour and family money. For each: what could happen, how likely it is, how bad it would be, and the safeguard. It ends with the hard limits (kill switch), what “normal” looks like, the launch checklist and what to do when something goes wrong.

The plan this document covers

  • Strategy: BB Breakout on 4h candles — close outside Bollinger (20, 2) in the direction of the 200 average; stop at the middle band; target 4× the stop distance; close after 8 days.
  • Coins: SOL and ZEC first; NEAR, ADA, GALA later.
  • Exchange: MEXC USDT-M perpetual futures, bot via the official API.
  • Money: $100 per coin. Risk per trade starts at $1–2; $5 only after the live record matches the backtest.
  • Bot: stop and target placed on MEXC right after every entry and never moved.

In one paragraph

The strategy will have losing streaks (up to ~12 in a row on SOL+ZEC), losing months (about 1 in 3) and drawdowns that take weeks or months to recover — these are certain, not failures. The real dangers are things we control: risking too much per trade, a position left without its stop, switching the bot off in the middle of a drawdown, scaling up before a live record exists, and exchange/API problems. Each has a safeguard below, and the kill switch on page 11 decides when to stop — not emotion.

S · Strategy and market risks

Risks that come from the strategy itself and from how markets behave. None of these can be removed — only sized and planned for.

S1Live results weaker than the backtestHighLikelihood: Certain · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
The 5 coins were chosen after seeing they did well. Picking coins blind with pre-2023 data, the average coin earned only +0.09R per trade from 2023 on, versus about +0.3R for SOL and ZEC. Expect live profit to be lower than the reports.
How we prevent it
  • Plan with +0.1R to +0.2R per trade, not +0.3R.
  • Start at $1–2 risk; scale up only after 6–12 months of live results.
  • Judge live results against the expected ranges on page 12, not against the backtest totals.
S2The edge fades or stops workingMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Major
What could happen
Edges decay. On other coins it already happened (ETH, DOGE, XRP faded after 2023). ADA is near break-even in 2026; ZEC made only +$0.86 (at $1 risk) in all of 2023; SOL+ZEC went from +0.40R per trade before 2023 to +0.32R after.
How we prevent it
  • Monthly review: rolling 12-month result per coin.
  • Pause a coin if its last 100 trades average below 0R, or its 12-month result is negative.
  • Never re-tune rules on recent losses (that is fitting to noise).
S3Long losing streaksMediumLikelihood: Certain · Impact: Minor
What could happen
Certain to happen. Longest in 6.6 years: 9 in a row on one coin (ZEC, ADA, GALA), 12 for SOL+ZEC together, 20 for all 5 coins. SOL+ZEC had 5+ losses in a row about every 3.6 months and 8+ about once a year.
How we prevent it
  • Fixed small risk per trade so a 12-loss streak costs about −12R ($12 at $1 risk).
  • Expect it; do not switch the bot off or change size during a streak.
  • Kill switch is set far beyond the worst streak (page 11).
S4Deep and slow drawdownsMediumLikelihood: Likely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Worst fall from a high: SOL+ZEC −14.6R, all 5 coins −25.6R. Recovery took a median of about 9 trades (~1 month) but up to 218 days (Oct 2022 → Jun 2023). Roughly 1 month in 3 is a loss even when the strategy works.
How we prevent it
  • Capital sized to absorb 2× the worst drawdown.
  • Warning and stop levels set in advance (page 11).
  • Evaluate on 6–12 months, never on one bad month.
S5Choppy, directionless marketsMediumLikelihood: Likely · Impact: Minor
What could happen
Most losing streaks happen in sideways markets: price breaks out of the band, then falls back to the middle band. Late 2022, early 2023 and December 2025 were the worst periods.
How we prevent it
  • Trading two (later five) coins spreads it out.
  • Filters were tested (whipsaw skip, break-even stops, loss-streak throttle) and all made results worse, so the rules accept chop rather than fight it.
S6Crash, gap or extreme candleLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
A sudden crash (like FTX in Nov 2022) can push price through the stop before it fills, so one loss is bigger than −1R. Worst single loss in the backtest: about −1.19R.
How we prevent it
  • Stops are stop-market orders that always fill.
  • Risk per trade kept small so a −2R or −3R outlier is survivable.
  • Cross margin with spare balance so a spike cannot liquidate the position before the stop.

S · Strategy and market risks (continued)

S7Coins losing together (correlation)MediumLikelihood: Likely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Crypto coins move together. With 5 coins, 20 losses in a row happened in only 19 days (Oct–Nov 2022). Up to 3 trades were open at the same time.
How we prevent it
  • Account-level kill switch on the combined balance, not per coin only.
  • Cap on how many trades can be open at once.
  • Add coins one at a time.
S8Backtest bias / overfittingMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Rules were tuned on 2019–2022 BTC, ETH and SOL, so SOL’s early years are in-sample. A backtest can look better than reality if rules are fitted to the past.
How we prevent it
  • Rules frozen since 20 Oct 2026; tested on 2023+ data and on coins never used for tuning.
  • No-look-ahead test: 0 mismatches in ~160,000 orders; independent bot replay matched SOL and ZEC trade for trade.
  • Live trades compared with the backtest replay every week.
S9Funding fee spikesLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Trivial
What could happen
Longs pay funding every 8 hours when rates are positive. Over 6.6 years funding cost −8.6R against +224.8R profit on SOL+ZEC; worst single trade −0.76R.
How we prevent it
  • Funding logged per trade.
  • Alert if the rate stays above 0.1% per 8 hours for several days.
  • Even 10× the historical funding would have left the strategy clearly profitable.
S10Trading costs riseLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Minor
What could happen
Your MEXC rates (ZEC 0%, SOL ~0.01%) can change. Results are not very sensitive: doubling fees and slippage keeps about 90% of the profit; 0.08% fees + 0.05% slippage still made +41% on SOL+ZEC in 2026 at $5 risk.
How we prevent it
  • Bot reads the fee rate from the API at start-up and logs it.
  • Monthly check of fees actually paid versus expected.

M · Money and position-size risks

Risks that come from how much is put in and how big each trade is. These are fully in our control.

M1Risk per trade too big for the accountHighLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Severe
What could happen
$5 risk on a $100 coin is 5% per trade. Starting at the worst moment, ZEC’s $100 would have dropped to about $22 before recovering. A bigger risk turns normal streaks into account-breaking losses.
How we prevent it
  • Start at $1–2 risk per trade on $100 per coin.
  • Raise risk only by the written rule on page 11 (after months of matching results).
  • Never above 5% of a coin’s starting balance per trade.
M2Order size rounding on MEXCMediumLikelihood: Certain · Impact: Minor
What could happen
MEXC trades SOL and ZEC in steps of about $11–13. At $1 risk a position is only $15–21, so real risk per trade swings between about $0.50 and $1.70.
How we prevent it
  • Use $2+ risk so rounding matters less.
  • Bot logs the real risk of every trade after rounding.
  • Skip a trade if rounding pushes risk above 1.5× the target.

M · Money and position-size risks (continued)

M3Liquidation before the stopLowLikelihood: Rare · Impact: Severe
What could happen
With too little margin or too much leverage, the exchange can close the position before the stop is reached and take the margin.
How we prevent it
  • Cross margin with the full $100 per coin behind positions of about $15–130.
  • Leverage setting low; bot checks the liquidation price is far beyond the stop before every entry.
M4Changing size on emotionMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Major
What could happen
Raising risk after wins (“it works!”) or after losses (“win it back faster”) is the most common way traders turn a working strategy into a loss.
How we prevent it
  • Risk is a setting in a config file, changed only at the monthly review, by the written rule.
  • Every change is logged with a date and reason.
M5Running costs bigger than profitLowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
At $1 risk, SOL+ZEC made about +$32 a year in the backtest. A rented server at about $5/month (~$60/year) would cost more than that.
How we prevent it
  • Start on a free option (home PC that stays on, or a free-tier server).
  • Move to a paid server only when the profit at the chosen risk clearly covers it.
M6Needing the money during a drawdownMediumLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Major
What could happen
Withdrawing in the middle of a drawdown locks in the loss and misses the recovery (recovery can take months).
How we prevent it
  • Only put in money that can stay untouched for 12 months.
  • Keep savings off the exchange.

E · Execution and technical risks

Risks from the bot, the server and the connection to the exchange. Most are prevented by how the bot is written; all are tested in practice mode and the small live phase.

E1Late entryLowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
The bot reacts seconds or minutes after the 4h candle closes and price has already moved, so the entry is worse than planned.
How we prevent it
  • Always-on machine with an accurate clock; runs a few seconds after each close (00, 04, 08, 12, 16, 20 UTC).
  • Skip if price has moved more than ¼ of the stop distance from the open, or already passed the stop or target.
  • Each trade logs planned vs actual entry so the cost of lateness is measured.
E2Missed signal (bot or internet down)LowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
A restart, power cut or internet loss at the candle close means a signal is missed or acted on very late.
How we prevent it
  • On restart the bot acts only on signals less than 15 minutes old.
  • Daily “still alive” message; alert if no heartbeat.
  • Open trades stay protected because stop and target sit on MEXC.

E · Execution and technical risks (continued)

E3Stop or target not placed after entryMediumLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Severe
What could happen
The worst technical failure: the entry fills but the stop order fails, leaving a position with no protection.
How we prevent it
  • Stop and target placed immediately after the fill, then read back from MEXC to confirm they exist.
  • Retry; if still missing after a few seconds, close the position at market and send an alert.
  • Checked again at every 4h run.
E4Duplicate ordersMediumLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Major
What could happen
A retry or restart sends the same order twice, opening two positions instead of one.
How we prevent it
  • Before any action the bot reads MEXC’s actual positions and open orders; it never trusts its own memory alone.
  • Unique order IDs per signal so MEXC rejects repeats.
  • One position per coin maximum, enforced in code.
E5Order rejectedLowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
Size below minimum, wrong position mode (MEXC error 7002), leverage or margin mismatch, insufficient balance.
How we prevent it
  • Start-up check of position mode (one-way), margin type and leverage.
  • Every rejection logged with the exchange message and sent as an alert.
E6Bad candle dataLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Acting on a candle that has not closed yet, a missing candle, or a wrong price from the API can create a false signal.
How we prevent it
  • Only fully closed candles are used (the forming one is dropped).
  • Gap and sanity checks before deciding; skip the run if data looks wrong.
E7Clock or time-zone mistakesLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Minor
What could happen
A wrong clock or time zone makes the bot check at the wrong time or misread which candle closed.
How we prevent it
  • All times in UTC.
  • Clock synced automatically; bot compares its time with MEXC server time at start-up.
E8Stop slippageLowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
In fast markets a stop-market order fills past the stop price. Normal slippage is already in the backtest; spikes are rarer and bigger.
How we prevent it
  • Logged per trade (planned vs actual exit).
  • Risk per trade small enough that a −1.5R loss is fine.

E · Execution and technical risks (continued)

E98-day time exit not executedLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Trivial
What could happen
If the bot is down when a trade reaches 48 candles, the position stays open longer than the rules say.
How we prevent it
  • Stop and target still protect it on MEXC.
  • On restart the bot closes any trade older than 48 candles immediately.
E10Partial fills and API timeoutsLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Minor
What could happen
An order only partly fills, or the API does not answer in time, so the bot does not know the true position.
How we prevent it
  • After every order the bot reads back the real position size and sizes stop/target to it.
  • Timeouts retried with backoff; rate limits respected.
E11Bot logic differs from the backtestLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
A bug makes the live bot take different trades than the tested strategy.
How we prevent it
  • Bot reuses the same calculation as the tested backtest.
  • Practice mode for 1–2 weeks: every signal compared with the backtest replay.
  • Weekly live-vs-replay check during the live phase.
E12Software or server breaksLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Minor
What could happen
A library update, full disk, crashed process or power cut stops the bot silently.
How we prevent it
  • Library versions pinned; updates tested in practice mode first.
  • Process auto-restarts; heartbeat alert if silent.

X · Exchange risks (MEXC)

Risks from depending on one exchange. Some can only be limited by keeping exposure small.

X1MEXC turns the futures API off againLowLikelihood: Unlikely · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
MEXC’s futures order API was “under maintenance” from July 2022 until 31 March 2026. It could happen again.
How we prevent it
  • Open trades stay protected by stop and target on MEXC.
  • Bot code written on a common library so it can move to Binance or Bybit by changing settings.
X2Outage or maintenanceLowLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Minor
What could happen
MEXC had a futures maintenance window on 25 June 2026. During an outage no new trades and no time exits happen.
How we prevent it
  • Bot retries after the outage and re-checks every open position.
  • Stop and target already on the exchange.

What could happen

O · Human and operational risks

Usually the biggest risks of all: what we do while the bot runs.

O1Turning the bot off in the middle of a drawdownHighLikelihood: Likely · Impact: Major
What could happen
Stopping after a losing streak locks in the loss and misses the recovery — the streak is when the strategy is most likely about to pay back.
How we prevent it
  • Stop only by the written kill-switch rule (page 11), never by feeling.
  • Re-read the “normal ranges” on page 12 before any decision.
O2Manual interferenceMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Closing winners early, moving stops, or taking extra trades by hand breaks the tested rules (the biggest profits come from trades held 6–8 days).
How we prevent it
  • No manual trading on the bot account.
  • Every manual action logged and reviewed.
O3Silent failure nobody noticesMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
The bot stops trading or trades wrongly for days without anyone looking.
How we prevent it
  • Telegram message on every entry, exit and error, plus a daily heartbeat.
  • Weekly 10-minute check (page 13).
O4Changing the rules after lossesMediumLikelihood: Possible · Impact: Moderate
What could happen
Adjusting settings after a bad month fits the strategy to noise and usually makes it worse.
How we prevent it
  • Rules frozen. Any change is tested on history first, then run in practice mode before it touches money.

When can risk go up?

Only at a monthly review, and only if all are true: at least 3 months live (≈ 25+ trades per coin); live trades match the backtest replay (same signals, slippage within expectations); the account is within the normal zone; and the step is small ($1 → $2 → $3 → $5, at least a month apart). After any kill-switch stop, risk goes back to $1.

Other normal facts

  • Win rate about 39% — most trades lose about −1R; about 1 in 6 hits the +4R target.
  • About 1 month in 3 is a loss; 15 of 46 months since 2023.
  • Average trade lasts about 4 days; the most profitable ones run the full 8 days.
  • Funding costs about 0.01–0.05R per trade; occasionally up to 0.76R on a long hold.

Signs something is actually wrong

  • Live trades do not match the replay (missing, extra or different trades).
  • Entries or stops consistently filling far from planned prices.
  • A position without its stop or target, even once.
  • Results below the “Bad (5%)” range for 12 weeks or more.
  • Any limit on page 11 reached.

What the bot journal records (every trade)

  • Signal candle, direction, planned entry/stop/target
  • Actual entry fill, size after rounding, real risk in $
  • Stop/target order IDs and confirmation time

  • Fees, funding payments, result in $ and R
  • Any errors, retries and restarts

Alerts that must reach your phone

Incident playbook — if this happens, do this

Written in advance so every problem has a calm, pre-decided answer.

SituationWhat to do
Position has no stop or targetBot closes it at market automatically. You: confirm on MEXC app it is closed; read the error; do not restart new entries until the cause is fixed.
Bot stopped / no heartbeatOpen trades are still protected on MEXC. Restart the bot; it rebuilds state from MEXC and closes any trade older than 8 days. Check the journal for missed signals.
MEXC down or in maintenanceDo nothing manually. Stops and targets stay on the exchange. After it is back, the bot re-checks every position.
MEXC disables the futures APIExisting trades finish via their stop/target. Stop new entries. Decide whether to move the bot to Binance or Bybit (settings change + new practice week).
Warning level reached (page 11)Keep the bot running at the same size. Check execution (replay match, slippage) first. Write down what the market is doing. No rule changes.
Kill switch reachedBot stops opening trades; open trades finish normally. Full review: replay match, costs, market regime. Restart only at $1 risk, and only if execution was correct.
Live trades differ from replayPause new entries. Find the cause (data, timing, rounding, bug). Fix, test in practice mode, then resume.
API key may be exposedDelete the key on MEXC immediately (open trades keep their stop/target). Create a new key with the same limits; check the account history.
Unexpected big loss on one tradeCheck whether it was a gap past the stop (normal in crashes) or an execution problem (late, wrong size, missing stop). Log it.
You feel like switching it offRe-read page 12. If no limit on page 11 is reached and execution matches the replay, the bot keeps running.

The three rules that matter most

  1. Never a position without its stop. The bot closes anything unprotected.
  2. Never change size or rules because of how a week felt. Only the monthly review, only by the written rules.
  3. Stop only at the kill switch. Everything before it is normal for this strategy.
